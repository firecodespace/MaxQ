Fidelity. Real: the public CelesTrak catalog and ISS orbit (TLEs, kilometre-level position error) and the orbital-mechanics math for windows and azimuth. Approximate: ascent is a 2D point-mass model with a simple atmosphere; rockets are fictional archetypes; rendezvous hours are impulsive estimates; screening thresholds are illustrative. In the 3D view, positions are to scale and computed, never drawn: Earth is the WGS-84 ellipsoid, satellites sit where SGP4 puts them, and a vehicle sits where the simulator puts it. Sizes are not all to scale — an object smaller than a pixel is drawn as a marker at a minimum on-screen size so it stays visible, and the panel says when something is drawn larger than life. Shapes are schematic: the catalog carries no geometry, so satellites, stations and debris are one model per class, and rocket dimensions are derived from each stage's own propellant mass, thrust and reference area rather than from any real vehicle. Earth imagery is NASA Blue Marble and night lights (public domain, 4,096 px wide, so about 10 km per pixel at the equator — it blurs on close approach). The star field is decorative and not a star catalog. MaxQ is an educational and portfolio project, not an operational planning tool. Anything marked DUMMY is placeholder data.
